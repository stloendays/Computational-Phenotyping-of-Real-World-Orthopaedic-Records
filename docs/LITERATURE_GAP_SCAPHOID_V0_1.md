# Literature Gap Audit — Scaphoid Treatment Composition v0.1

**Purpose:** define what the current fixed dataset can contribute after explicitly accounting for what the scaphoid-nonunion literature already establishes.

**Status:** literature-positioning document; it does not change the frozen primary outcome.

## 1. What is already established

### 1.1 Internal fixation plus grafting is conventional nonunion treatment

Contemporary reviews already state that scaphoid nonunion frequently requires internal fixation with supplemental bone graft, with graft source and vascularity selected according to proximal-pole viability, deformity, vascular compromise, and other lesion-specific factors.

Key source:
- Ayalon O, Rettig SA, Tedesco LJ. *Bone Graft and Fixation Options in the Surgical Management of Scaphoid Nonunion*. J Am Acad Orthop Surg. 2024. PMID: 39531592. doi:10.5435/JAAOS-D-24-00510.

**Implication for this paper:** we cannot claim novelty for the generic observation that nonunion surgery uses bone graft.

### 1.2 Graft choice is heterogeneous and no single graft technique is universally superior

A 2024 umbrella review found no consistent global superiority of vascularized versus nonvascularized grafting, while lesion characteristics such as proximal-pole avascular necrosis, revision status, humpback deformity, and instability modified the clinical context in which a technique may be preferred.

Key source:
- *Graft choice for managing scaphoid non-union: umbrella review*. 2024. PMID: 39122186.

A 2024 survey of hand surgeons similarly reported substantial variation in graft choice for scaphoid nonunion, revision surgery, and avascular necrosis despite near-unanimous use of headless compression screws in many scaphoid fixation settings.

Key source:
- Strelzow JA, et al. *Scaphoid Fractures and Nonunion: A Survey-based Review of Hand Surgeon's Practice and the Evidence*. 2024. PMID: 39703594. doi:10.1016/j.jhsg.2024.06.013.

**Implication:** variation in graft selection is already known. The local study should not be presented as discovering practice variation itself.

### 1.3 Time from injury/chronicity is already biologically and clinically relevant

Prior literature explicitly connects increasing time from injury with more complex reconstructive decision-making and/or worse outcomes.

Examples:
- *Scaphoid Reconstruction*. 2019. PMID: 31739880. The review describes increasingly complex surgical strategies with time from injury and notes that selected nonunions less than one year from trauma without carpal malalignment may be treated with percutaneous screw fixation.
- *The outcome of bone graft surgery for nonunion of fractures of the scaphoid*. Multicentre retrospective study, 2019. The interval from acute fracture to nonunion surgery was adversely associated with union outcome in adjusted analysis. PMCID: PMC6696740.
- *Arthroscopically Assisted Bone Grafting Reduces Union Time of Scaphoid Nonunions Compared to Percutaneous Screw Fixation Alone*. The compared minimally invasive groups differed strongly in injury-to-surgery interval, illustrating the close relationship between chronicity and treatment selection. PMCID: PMC7000248.
- *Necessity of grafting in scaphoid nonunion fixation: A comparative outcome analysis*. 2026. PMID: 41695733. Graft-less fixation remained viable in selected stable nonunions, whereas graft augmentation was reported as particularly advantageous in cystic lesions, larger bone loss, and nonunions of at least one year duration.

**Implication:** the secondary duration analysis cannot be described as discovering that chronicity matters. Its contribution is limited to testing whether a duration gradient is visible in the operative-composition choices represented in this local real-world record set.

### 1.4 Large datasets have already examined utilization and graft-related practice

A 2025 claims-based study of 1,324 scaphoid-nonunion operations found variation in MRI use and an association between recent MRI and subsequent vascularized bone graft use (10.6% vs 4.7%).

Key source:
- Shapiro LM, et al. *MRI for Scaphoid Nonunion: Utilization Rates, Factors Associated With Utilization, and Subsequent Vascularized Bone Graft Use*. J Hand Surg Am. 2025;50(2):182-187. PMID: 39614839. doi:10.1016/j.jhsa.2024.10.008.

**Implication:** this paper cannot claim to be the first real-world analysis of graft utilization in scaphoid nonunion.

## 2. What appears less directly addressed

The literature located for this audit is dominated by four study types:

1. technique/outcome studies **within already-established nonunion cohorts**;
2. graft-type comparisons;
3. prognostic studies of union after nonunion surgery;
4. surveys or claims analyses of practice variation/utilization.

The present dataset supports a different, narrower comparison:

> **Across physician-adjudicated wrist-scaphoid presentation states, which component of operative treatment changes: the mechanical fixation base, or the addition of biological/reconstructive augmentation?**

The preliminary local pattern is a dissociation:

- internal fixation is nearly invariant across operative presentation groups;
- graft augmentation is the component that changes.

This `mechanical base versus biological augmentation` decomposition is the most defensible paper-level contribution from the current data.

It should be presented as an **observational treatment-composition result**, not as a new treatment algorithm.

## 3. Secondary contribution: heterogeneity within established chronic/nonunion

The duration secondary hypothesis asks:

> Within physician-confirmed established chronic/nonunion operative records, is graft augmentation concentrated among records with longer documented wrist-related disease duration?

This is potentially useful because it moves the paper beyond a simple acute-versus-nonunion contrast and examines heterogeneity **inside established disease**.

However, chronicity is not a novel risk concept. The allowed contribution is:

- physician-validated recovery of duration from routine clinical documentation;
- comparison of the observed continuous duration distribution by graft status;
- demonstration, if preserved after Gold adjudication, that local real-world treatment composition follows a clinically plausible chronicity gradient.

The paper must not derive or recommend a treatment threshold from the local sample.

## 4. Novelty statements that are allowed

Preferred language:

> Previous studies have largely compared graft techniques and outcomes within established scaphoid-nonunion cohorts. We instead examined the composition of documented surgery across real-world wrist-scaphoid presentation states, separating a common mechanical fixation component from additional biological augmentation.

> The analysis further explored whether documented wrist-related disease duration differentiated graft-positive from graft-negative operations within established chronic/nonunion presentations.

> These findings describe real-world treatment composition and do not establish a causal treatment-selection rule.

## 5. Novelty statements that are not allowed

Do **not** claim:

- this is the first study to examine bone graft use in scaphoid nonunion;
- this is the first real-world study of scaphoid nonunion treatment;
- we discovered that chronicity affects treatment complexity;
- duration is a new indication for grafting;
- grafting is superior in chronic disease;
- the local duration distribution identifies an optimal clinical threshold;
- the study provides a validated treatment algorithm.

## 6. Consequence for article structure

The paper should foreground the clinical treatment-composition result rather than computational methodology.

Recommended Results order:

1. physician-defined cohort and operative denominator;
2. **internal fixation versus bone-graft augmentation by presentation state**;
3. component-count / broader augmentation sensitivity;
4. **duration distribution by graft status within established chronic/nonunion**;
5. measurement validation only as a short methodological support section or supplement.

Recommended Discussion order:

1. fixation remained a common mechanical base while augmentation changed;
2. relation to existing nonunion treatment algorithms and practice variation;
3. duration-related heterogeneity as a secondary, clinically plausible observation;
4. limits from missing imaging severity, small sample, single centre, and data-derived hypothesis generation.

## 7. Publication-level assessment

With the current fixed data, the strongest realistic positioning is a **focused exploratory retrospective clinical study / brief report** rather than a high-capacity predictive or causal study.

Publication value depends on three conditions:

1. physician Gold preserves the graft-augmentation signal;
2. the effect is not driven by one or two ambiguous records in leave-one-out/influence analyses;
3. the duration secondary signal remains directionally coherent after physician adjudication.

If these conditions fail, the paper should be downgraded to a descriptive EHR cohort report rather than rescued by adding an LLM or post-hoc composite endpoint.
