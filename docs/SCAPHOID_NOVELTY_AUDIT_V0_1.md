# Scaphoid Novelty Audit v0.1

**Purpose:** define what is and is not novel about the focused manuscript before physician Gold is completed.

This document is a claim-boundary audit, not a systematic review.

## 1. What the literature already establishes

The manuscript must **not** present the following as novel findings.

### A. Scaphoid nonunion commonly requires internal fixation and may require bone graft

A 2024 JAAOS review explicitly describes internal fixation as a common mechanical treatment component and supplemental grafting as a strategy selected according to proximal-pole viability, blood supply and deformity.

Reference:

Ayalon O, Rettig SA, Tedesco LJ. *Bone Graft and Fixation Options in the Surgical Management of Scaphoid Nonunion*. J Am Acad Orthop Surg. 2024. PMID 39531592. doi:10.5435/JAAOS-D-24-00510.

### B. No single graft strategy is universally superior

A 2024 umbrella review of nine systematic reviews/meta-analyses found no consistent global superiority of vascularized over nonvascularized grafting, while noting that lesion characteristics such as avascular necrosis, revision status and deformity influence graft selection and outcomes.

Reference:

Baamir A, et al. *Graft choice for managing scaphoid non-union: umbrella review*. Hand Surg Rehabil. 2024;43(4):101759. PMID 39122186. doi:10.1016/j.hansur.2024.101759.

### C. Surgeon practice is much more consistent for fixation than for graft selection

A 2024 ASSH survey reported nearly unanimous preference for headless compression screw fixation in common scaphoid settings, whereas graft choice for nonunion, revision cases and avascular necrosis showed substantial variation.

Reference:

Strelzow JA, et al. *Scaphoid Fractures and Nonunion: A Survey-based Review of Hand Surgeon's Practice and the Evidence*. J Hand Surg Glob Online. 2024;6(6):836-841. PMID 39703594. doi:10.1016/j.jhsg.2024.06.013.

### D. Graft-less fixation can be appropriate in selected nonunions

The literature already contains fixation-without-graft series and comparative work showing that grafting is not mandatory in every stable nonunion.

A 2026 comparative analysis reported that graft augmentation was particularly advantageous in cystic nonunions, lesions with at least 5 mm bone loss, and nonunions of at least 1 year duration, while graft-less fixation remained viable in selected stable lesions.

Reference:

*Necessity of grafting in scaphoid nonunion fixation: A comparative outcome analysis*. 2026. PMID 41695733.

Therefore the manuscript cannot claim that it discovered that long-standing nonunion may be more likely to receive or benefit from grafting.

## 2. What the current dataset may still contribute

The potentially publishable contribution is narrower and observational.

### Contribution 1 — treatment-composition decomposition across presentation states

The study asks whether the transition from **acute/new fracture** to **established chronic/nonunion** is reflected primarily in the addition of biological/reconstructive augmentation while internal fixation remains a common mechanical base.

This is conceptually different from simply reporting:

> nonunion patients receive more bone graft.

The intended result structure is:

- fixation prevalence in acute/new;
- fixation prevalence in established chronic/nonunion;
- bone-graft prevalence in acute/new;
- bone-graft prevalence in established chronic/nonunion;
- effect estimate for graft augmentation;
- explicit comparison showing whether fixation remains relatively stable while augmentation changes.

The manuscript should use language such as **operative composition**, **mechanical base**, and **biological augmentation**, not language implying a new treatment indication.

### Contribution 2 — real-world documentation rather than a selected nonunion surgical cohort

Much of the published graft literature starts with patients already defined as scaphoid nonunion and then compares graft techniques, fixation techniques, or healing outcomes.

The current study instead reconstructs presentation state from routine clinical records and compares documented operative composition across physician-confirmed acute/new and established chronic/nonunion states.

This is an observational practice-pattern question, not an efficacy comparison.

### Contribution 3 — duration heterogeneity within established chronic/nonunion

If physician adjudication preserves the current duration signal, the study can report that graft augmentation within established chronic/nonunion was concentrated among records with longer documented wrist-related disease duration.

The novelty is **not** that a one-year threshold predicts graft benefit. The 2026 literature already supports long duration as one relevant treatment characteristic.

The local contribution would be a small real-world descriptive observation of treatment heterogeneity using physician-adjudicated routine documentation.

## 3. Claims that are allowed if physician Gold supports them

### Strongest allowable primary claim

> In physician-confirmed operative wrist-scaphoid records, established chronic/nonunion presentation was associated with greater documented bone-graft augmentation than acute/new fracture, while internal fixation remained common in both groups.

### Allowable secondary duration claim

> Within established chronic/nonunion operative records, graft augmentation was concentrated among records with longer physician-adjudicated wrist-related disease duration.

### Allowable methodological claim

> Broad “舟骨” retrieval required physician-level anatomical disambiguation because routine Chinese documentation included both wrist scaphoid and foot navicular records, and same-admission operative-note availability did not guarantee that the documented procedure treated the wrist scaphoid.

These methodological observations support cohort validity but should not replace the clinical question as the main scientific message.

---

## 4. Claims that are not allowed

Do not claim:

- bone graft is superior to fixation alone;
- chronicity causes graft selection;
- duration causes a need for grafting;
- one year is a validated treatment threshold in this cohort;
- bone graft improves union in this dataset;
- the study predicts future nonunion;
- the study identifies optimal surgery;
- the study externally validates a treatment algorithm;
- computational phenotyping itself is the main clinical discovery.

The available data do not contain the longitudinal outcomes or standardized lesion-severity variables required for those claims.

---

## 5. Novelty risk assessment

### Risk: high if framed as

> Bone graft is used more often in scaphoid nonunion.

This is clinically expected and well represented in existing reviews.

### Risk: moderate if framed as

> Chronic/nonunion presentations receive more complex surgery.

This is broader but still unsurprising and may be criticized as descriptive.

### Best current framing

> **Operative treatment composition across acute/new versus established chronic/nonunion wrist-scaphoid presentations: fixation as a common mechanical base with differential biological augmentation.**

This framing is narrower, falsifiable, directly supported by the available records, and aligned with known practice variation in graft selection.

---

## 6. Publication positioning

Given the small operative sample and fixed retrospective source, the project should be positioned as a:

- focused exploratory retrospective study;
- observational practice-pattern / treatment-composition study;
- brief clinical research report if required by the target journal.

It should not be marketed as a large AI study or as definitive comparative effectiveness research.

The physician Gold result will determine whether the clinical signal is strong enough for a stand-alone paper. If the primary association materially attenuates after adjudication, the study should be reported as a negative or measurement-sensitive result rather than rescued by changing the endpoint.
